Pitch like a first date: how to pitch to investors

Nobody proposes on a first date. Yet many founders walk into an investor meeting trying to close in forty minutes. The goal of a first pitch is simpler: earn the second meeting.

Slavomír Hruška · 20 Oct 2026 · 6 min read

Why fundraising is like dating

Both start with a first impression and a lot of rejection. Both reward confidence and punish neediness. And in both, impressing someone at the start is simple, but proving you're worth sticking with takes work. Thinking of a raise as a series of relationships, rather than a series of transactions, changes how you prepare.

The first five minutes

Investors form a view fast. Start with something human and specific: why this problem, why you, why now. Skip the slide that explains the market they already know. A clear, confident opening buys you attention for everything that follows.

Curiosity beats completeness

A pitch that answers every question leaves nothing to talk about. Leave room for the investor to lean in and ask. Questions are the best sign of interest you'll get, and they turn a presentation into a conversation. That conversation is where founder–investor chemistry is built.

Reading the signals

Real interest
  • They ask about your team and hiring plans
  • They propose a concrete next step
  • They introduce you to a partner
Politeness
  • "Keep us posted on progress"
  • "It's a bit early for us"
  • "We'll think about it"

Chemistry is built in the follow-up

The best founders treat the follow-up as the second date. Send what you promised, when you promised it. Share a short update when something real happens. Consistency is how an investor learns you're worth sticking with.

Before your next pitch
  • Define the one next step you want from this meeting.
  • Rehearse a 60-second opening about why you.
  • Cut one slide that explains something they already know.
  • Prepare the follow-up email before you walk in.
