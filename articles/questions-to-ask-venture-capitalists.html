Questions to ask venture capitalists in a first meeting

Slavomír Hruška · 9 Nov 2026 · 8 min read
Short answer: The best questions reveal investment fit, the fund's decision process, its concerns about your company and what it takes to move forward. Ask a few relevant questions well, rather than reading through an interrogation checklist.

Founders often spend all their preparation time thinking about answers. That's understandable—but an investor meeting is a two-way assessment. You need to learn whether the fund can invest, whether the partner might champion your company and whether working together for years would make sense.

Questions about investor fit

  1. What attracted you to this company? Learn which part of the story created the meeting.
  2. What stage do you most often enter at? A firm that says it invests in seed might mainly write Series A checks.
  3. What is your usual initial check size, and do you lead rounds? Avoid spending a month courting a follower when you need a lead.
  4. Which investments have you made in this space, and what did you learn? Listen for relevant experience rather than name-dropping.
  5. Are there sector, geography or ownership constraints we should know about? This can uncover a mismatch early.

Questions about how they decide

  1. What would need to be true for you to champion this internally? Helps surface an actual threshold.
  2. Who else participates in the decision? A positive first meeting may still require partnership approval.
  3. What does your process usually look like after a first conversation? Ask about steps, not promises.
  4. What are the main reasons deals like ours don't get approved? Often more useful than asking whether they like the idea.
  5. What information would help you make an informed decision? Lets you prepare material relevant to their uncertainties.

Questions about the company

  1. Which assumption in our plan would you challenge first? Tests where conviction is fragile.
  2. How would you explain our differentiation to a partner who wasn't here? A practical test of message clarity.
  3. Where do you see the biggest execution risk? Helps you distinguish a narrative problem from a business problem.
  4. Are we measuring the traction you would care most about? Investors may focus on retention, expansion, revenue quality or technical proof depending on the market.
  5. What important evidence do you think we're missing? Generates an action item whether or not the investor proceeds.

Questions about the relationship

  1. How do you work with founders when something goes wrong? Useful answers include examples, not slogans.
  2. What does a productive relationship between partner and founder look like to you? Checks expectations about involvement and governance.
  3. What specific next step would make sense after this meeting? The most operationally important question of all.

How to choose your five

If you have only 30 minutes, don't ask all 18. Choose one to establish fit, one to understand the decision process, two to reveal risks and one to confirm the next step. Adapt to the discussion. If the investor already explained how the committee works, move on to a different question.

A better closing conversation

Instead of ending with “so, what do you think?”, try:

“Based on what you've seen, is there enough fit to explore this further? If so, what would you need to see next, and who would be involved?”

It's direct without cornering the investor. A “no” with a clear reason can be more valuable than an indefinite “maybe”.

Interpret the answer, not the mood

Investors can be warm and still decline. They can be skeptical and still pursue diligence. Watch what happens: requests for material, specific questions, the introduction of colleagues, follow-up dates and clear mandates. Use those as operating signals—not a prediction of a term sheet.

Frequently asked questions

Should I ask the VC about their fund size? Yes, if relevant to check size, ownership and follow-on capacity. Ask professionally and connect the question to your financing needs.

Should I ask if they will invest during the first meeting? You can ask about fit and next steps. Demanding an immediate commitment usually ignores how partnerships make decisions.

What if the investor avoids all process questions? Record the uncertainty. Continue your process and seek more clarity later if a credible reason emerges.
