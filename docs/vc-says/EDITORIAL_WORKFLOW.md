# Editorial and localization workflow

## Voice and positioning

The Fundraising Game's distinctive premise is decoding the human dynamics of VC fundraising using memorable relationship metaphors. Keep the playful voice, but ensure it remains founder-friendly, professional and inclusive. A useful tool should never humiliate founders or tell them that rejection is their fault. Do not turn a socially tactful message into a factually certain psychological diagnosis.

## Review pipeline

1. **Evidence collection.** For each signal, research the actual phrase and related fundraising process. Cite source URLs and what they support. Public commentary is background evidence, NOT a basis for asserting what a particular investor secretly thinks.
2. **English editorial draft.** Write at least two plausible meanings for ambiguous phrases, plus contextual clues, a clarifier, concrete next step, short natural reply and hypothetical example.
3. **Localization.** Localize search phrasing and narrative to SK, CS, DE and PL. Translate meaning, not only syntax. Note terms with no clean local equivalent and keep conventional industry English terms where founders use them.
4. **Independent review.** A native or highly fluent reviewer checks completeness, grammar, professional tone, jargon, local applicability and context. Avoid stereotyped country generalizations.
5. **Fact and permission check.** Named investor quotes, interview extracts and actual case studies require accurate records, explicit attribution and permission appropriate to publication. Unverified quotes must not ship. Verify site author's numeric claims separately.
6. **Technical checks.** Validate every locale variant has title/description, visible main content, internal links, correct locale output and accurate source displays.
7. **Publication.** Mark each locale `published` only if approved, and update sitemap/hreflang at release. Track date and revision. Leave partial variants as draft/noindex and out of hreflang groups.
8. **Refresh.** Revisit high-traffic entries when evidence changes or readers submit valid corrections. Maintain revision history.

## Example analysis: "You're too early"

**Possible meaning A:** The fund typically invests later or with bigger checks than this startup's current stage.

**Possible meaning B:** The investor wants greater evidence of customer adoption, retention, technical validation, team strength or another specific risk reduction.

**Possible meaning C:** It can be a polite decline without a concrete reconsideration plan.

**What to observe:** Does the investor specify a milestone and a follow-up time? Do they introduce another investor? Do they request a progress update or another meeting? Concrete action is stronger evidence than enthusiasm alone.

**Clarifier:** "Thanks for the candid feedback. Which milestone would make it worth revisiting this—customer traction, validation or something else?"

**Next action:** Record the feedback, compare it against the investor's thesis and publicly known investment stage, pursue better-fit funds now and re-engage with relevant progress later.

**Short reply:** "Thanks for taking a look. To make sure I focus on the right milestones, is there one specific proof point you'd need to see before a future conversation?"

**Sources:** Use the source list in the start file; label this as editorial synthesis, not a quotation from an interview.

## Example analysis: "Let's keep in touch"

**Possible meanings:** (1) Genuine long-term interest but not a commitment now; (2) a polite soft no; (3) openness to milestone updates. Do not treat any as certain.

**Signals:** A precise request for the next update or a concrete future call is stronger than a generic positive comment.

**Clarifier:** "Happy to. What development would be most relevant for me to share, and would an update after the next milestone be useful?"

**Action:** Offer a high-signal update only when meaningful progress occurs; do not spam weekly check-ins.

## Market-specific content rules

- Do not imply all Polish/German/Slovak/Czech investors behave alike.
- Country sections may explain differences in financing instruments, institutions or terminology only when backed by current market-specific primary sources.
- A startup raising from Berlin and Bratislava can use the same behavioral decoding framework; content differences should be sourced and useful, not artificially introduced for SEO.
- Founder/private communication is confidential. An anonymous story still requires consent if identifiable.

## Search-led editorial roadmap

Cluster 1: rejection and ambiguous feedback (`too early`, `not a fit`, `need traction`, `circle back`, `ghosting`).
Cluster 2: strong and weak positive signals (`second meeting`, `partner intro`, `customer reference request`, `financial model request`).
Cluster 3: process (`IC`, `lead investor`, `valuation`, `ticket size`, `due diligence`).
Cluster 4: practical response writing (`follow up`, `what to ask VC`, `update email`, `post-rejection reply`).

Use Search Console and country-level Keyword Planner to determine real priorities and page titles. Do not claim numerical demand until measured. Do not publish 30 virtually identical glossaries merely because there are 30 seed phrases.
