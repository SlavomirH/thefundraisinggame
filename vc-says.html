VC Says vs. What It Means

Decode investor feedback without overthinking it. Common phrases, possible interpretations, signals to watch, and an exact next step.

10 entries shown

  • Soft noStage

    You're too early for us.

    Usually a stage or evidence gap, sometimes a polite pass. The difference shows up in whether the investor names a specific milestone and invites an update — so ask for that milestone, keep raising from earlier-stage investors, and come back only with real progress.

    See how to respond →

  • Hard noThesis & fit

    It's not the right fit for us.

    Most often a clear no tied to the fund's mandate — sector, stage, geography or cheque size — and occasionally a polite way to express doubts about the market. Either way, it is rarely worth arguing. Ask once which dimension didn't fit, ask for a referral if the conversation was warm, and move on.

    See how to respond →

  • Soft noTiming

    Let's keep in touch.

    An open door, not a commitment. It can mean genuine long-term interest, interest that is waiting for a specific signal, or a gentle no. A precise request for a particular update is a stronger signal than a warm but generic sign-off.

    See how to respond →

  • AmbiguousTiming

    Circle back next quarter.

    A timing message that can be real (fund pacing, partner bandwidth, their own fundraising) or a soft pass that uses time as the reason. What separates them is whether the investor says what would be different next quarter and agrees to a date.

    See how to respond →

  • AmbiguousProcess

    Send me your deck.

    Neutral on its own. It is usually an initial screening step and sometimes a sign of real early interest; what matters is what it follows and what happens after. Send a concise, current deck promptly and propose a specific next step.

    See how to respond →

  • PositiveProcess

    Let's schedule another meeting.

    A positive process signal: the investor is spending more time on you. It is still not a commitment. The strongest versions come with an agenda, a date, more senior attendees or specific data requests.

    See how to respond →

  • PositiveProcess

    I'd like you to meet my partner.

    Usually a positive process signal: your contact is willing to spend internal capital on you. It may be a champion building support, a required second opinion, or a specialist check. Prepare for fund-level questions, and ask your contact what the partner will care about.

    See how to respond →

  • Soft noTraction

    We need to see more traction.

    A real evidence gap, a stage mismatch, or a general objection that sits on top of a different concern. Ask which metric matters and at roughly what level — then decide whether that milestone fits your own plan before chasing it.

    See how to respond →

  • Soft noRound & terms

    Come back when you have a lead investor.

    Conditional interest at best — and sometimes a polite no. Some investors genuinely only follow; others use it to wait for someone else's validation. Count it as conditional, never as committed, and ask what cheque size and conditions would apply.

    See how to respond →

  • Soft noObserved behaviourTiming

    No response after two follow-ups.

    Silence is a behaviour, not a message, so read it cautiously. It can be bandwidth, a lost email, a stalled internal process — or low interest. After two unanswered follow-ups, send one brief close-the-loop note and redirect your energy to active conversations.

    See how to respond →

See the rules the decoder uses

Each library entry lists 2–3 possible interpretations, each tagged as interest, conditional, mismatch, polite pass or logistics/noise. These rules move them up or down; ties keep the editorial order.

  • R1 — A specific date or meeting was offered → genuine-interest and conditional readings move up; polite-pass moves down.
  • R2 — They requested specific materials → interest/conditional readings move up.
  • R3 — They brought in another partner from their own fund → process/interest readings move up.
  • R4 — They suggested a different fund → fit/mandate mismatch moves up.
  • R5 — No next step at all → polite-pass reading moves up.
  • R6 — Only a vague next step (“keep us posted”) → conditional and polite-pass readings move up slightly.
  • R7 — More than three weeks with no concrete next step → polite-pass moves up.
  • R8 — A promised date has passed → stalled-process/bandwidth and polite-pass readings move up.
  • R9 — Less than a week has passed → too soon to read much into timing; logistics readings move up slightly.
  • R10 — Pre-seed + “too early”/“traction” → many funds start later, so stage mismatch moves up slightly.
