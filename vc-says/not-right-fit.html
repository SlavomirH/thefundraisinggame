“It’s not the right fit for us” — what it might mean and how to reply

Hard noThesis & fit

Editorial guidance · Updated 2026-11-09

Most often a clear no tied to the fund's mandate — sector, stage, geography or cheque size — and occasionally a polite way to express doubts about the market. Either way, it is rarely worth arguing. Ask once which dimension didn't fit, ask for a referral if the conversation was warm, and move on.

What they said

It's not the right fit for us.

Also phrased as: “It doesn't fit our thesis” · “This is outside our focus” · “We don't invest in this space” · “Not a fit for our fund”

What it might mean

Several readings are plausible. Context decides — not the words alone.

  • A genuine mandate mismatch: sector, stage, geography, cheque size or ownership target sits outside what the fund can do.Clues: They name the constraint; their portfolio and website confirm it; the decline comes quickly, sometimes before a meeting.
  • A conviction gap expressed politely — they don't believe in the market size, the approach or the timing.Clues: They invest in adjacent companies at your stage; the meeting included sceptical questions about the market; no specific constraint is named.
  • A portfolio conflict — they already back a company that overlaps with yours.Clues: A similar company appears in their portfolio; the reasoning is vague; they decline after learning more about your positioning.

What strengthens or weakens the signal

What strengthens the signal

  • They explain precisely which part of the mandate doesn't fit — useful for targeting.
  • They suggest a better-fit fund or offer an introduction.
  • They say what would need to change for a future conversation (rare, but useful).

What weakens the signal

  • A generic template email with no reason.
  • No reply to a polite request for the reason — treat that as final.
  • They emphasise how busy they are rather than anything about your company.

What to ask

Thanks for the clear answer — it genuinely helps. Was it mainly stage, sector or geography? It will help me focus on better-fit investors.

What to do next

Ask the clarifying question once, then move this investor out of your active pipeline. Use what you learn to tighten your target list (stage, sector, geography, cheque size). If the relationship was warm, ask for one referral.

Follow-up timing

Reply once within a couple of business days. Don't follow up again this round; revisit only if their thesis or your company changes materially.

Example (hypothetical)

Hypothetical — not a real company or investor.

Hypothetical: a hardware-plus-software startup pitches a fund that only invests in pure software. The associate replies “not the right fit for us” the same day. The mandate-mismatch reading is most plausible. The founder replies once asking whether hardware was the issue, gets a one-line confirmation, and removes hardware-averse funds from the target list.
